# Heart Health Co. — house style

Derived from the 16 Production Briefs (Hip Therapy Belt). This is what new work has to read
like. When a draft drifts from this, say where and why before shipping it.

> Source status: Groups A–F complete, Group G partial — 9 of 16 briefs seen. The full document
> should be dropped into this folder. Patterns below hold across all 9 and are unlikely to move,
> but the remaining 7 may add angles and formats not listed here.

---

## The one sentence the whole brand runs on

**Nothing was reaching the joint.**

Every brief arrives at this line or a version of it. It is the mechanism, the villain, and the
belief shift in five words. Depth is the product. Everything else on the market treats a layer
above the problem.

Do not restate it as a claim. Earn it by listing what failed first, then name the layer each one
stopped at.

## Voice

**First person, past tense, one woman.** Never brand voice. Never "you deserve." The reader
recognises herself in someone else's account rather than being addressed by a company.

**Short declaratives, then one long one.** Fragments are used deliberately and often. "Sharp.
Instant." / "Support. Not fix. At $150 a session." The rhythm is choppy on purpose — it is how
the coffee shop test passes.

**Concrete objects over abstractions, always.** The brand never says "reduced mobility." It says
dust on the top shelf, the biscuit tin moved down, two minutes and a strategy for socks, a
pillow between the knees, the blister pack that hasn't been popped in three weeks.

**Specific times and numbers everywhere.** 3:17am. Ordered at 3:30am. Woke at 6:14am. $6,732 in
premiums. Eighty-seven comments. 152 days. The specificity is what makes it credible — a round
number reads invented.

**Understatement carries the emotion.** "It felt stupid to complain about socks." "That's how I
measure what my hip has cost me. In dust." The copy never tells the reader to feel something.

**Australian and plain.** Panadol, Medicare, GP, physio, cortisone, the public list. Local words,
no Americanisms.

## Structure

Every long primary text follows the same arc:

1. **A dated, specific opening moment.** "The letter came on a Tuesday." "3am. Eyes open." "I
   found out on a Thursday afternoon."
2. **The slow accumulation.** Losses stack one at a time, each dismissed as small.
3. **The list she writes at 3am.** The moment she sees the total.
4. **What everyone told her** — GP, physio, daughter — and the question none of them answered.
5. **The outside voice.** A retired physio at a barbecue, a forum post, her own arithmetic in a
   car park. Never the brand explaining.
6. **The mechanism, in one plain paragraph.** Past the skin, past the muscle, to the joint.
7. **Bought it sceptically, because of the guarantee.** Skepticism is stated, never argued with.
8. **Week two, week three, week four, week six.** Progress noticed in retrospect, never
   announced. "I only realised afterwards what I'd done."
9. **A closing line that hands the decision back.** "Your surgeon will still be there in thirty
   days. You might not need to be."

Short version is the same arc compressed to five paragraphs. It keeps the opening moment, the
list of failures, the mechanism, one result, and the closing line.

## Rules the briefs never break

- **Concede what is true about the alternatives.** The physio is lovely. The exercises are good.
  The doctors are correct but incomplete. Never attack the medical system — reframe it.
- **Never position as a cure or a surgery replacement.** "Not a replacement for surgery. A
  replacement for waiting in pain." This is both the honest framing and the compliant one.
- **Name the drawer.** The objection that she has bought things off the internet before is
  raised by the copy itself, in her words, before she can raise it.
- **Guarantee appears in every piece.** 30-day money-back is the permission to try, not a
  footnote.
- **"Engineered in Sydney" closes, near the guarantee.** Local credibility in an import
  category.
- **Progress is behavioural, never a pain score.** Socks, the shelf, the car, the side she sleeps
  on. Never "pain reduced by 40%."

## Statics

One headline, one subhead, callouts, one CTA bar — shared across all three image variants so the
test isolates visual treatment, not copy.

Formats in use, matched deliberately to the angle rather than chosen for variety:

| Angle type | Format | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| System / waitlist | Escalation Ladder | Shows the missing step in a ladder that only goes one way |
| Social proof | Testimonial Quote Card | She needs peer permission, not product claims |
| Cost and insurance | Cost Breakdown | The arithmetic is the argument |
| Daily loss / transformation | Before/After Split | The change is measured in moments |
| Sleep | Problem Agitation, night palette | She is scrolling at 3am — match the moment |

Variant discipline: A is the format's primary visual, B is product-forward, C is stripped back
to text to test whether the headline alone carries. 3 variants × 9:16 and 4:5 = 6 assets a brief.

## What would count as drift

- Brand voice, second person, or any sentence beginning "Imagine."
- A pain scale, a percentage improvement, or a benefit list.
- Round or unsourced numbers where a specific one belongs.
- Attacking doctors, physios or Medicare rather than reframing them.
- Uniform sentence length — the clearest tell that a draft was not written in this voice.
- A format chosen because it looks different, rather than because the angle calls for it.
