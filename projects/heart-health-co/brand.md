## Product facts

- 4-in-1 therapy in one wearable band: heat, vibration, red light, infrared.
- Wearable belt worn over the hip joint. Hands-free.
- Session length: 20 minutes. Daily use. No per-session cost, no annual limit.

## Mechanism

**Depth.** Infrared travels past skin and muscle to reach the hip joint itself. Heat and
vibration support from the outside; red light and infrared do the deep work.

The mechanism is defined against what fails. Everything else in the category treats a layer
above the joint:

| What she has tried | Why it fails |
| --- | --- |
| Heat pad | Warms the skin. Cold by midnight. Never reaches the joint |
| Creams | Sit on the surface |
| Physio exercises | Strengthen the muscles *around* the joint, not the joint |
| Panadol | Masks the pain signal. Wears off by 2am |
| Cortisone | Reduces swelling ~3 weeks, then the joint swells again |

The one-line version used throughout: *nothing was reaching the joint.*

## ICP

Australian woman, 50–65.

- **Identity** — has done everything alone her whole adult life. Independence is the thing she
  is protecting. Dependence reads as surrender, not recovery.
- **Belief** — she has been told it is her age, or inflammation, or that she is doing too much.
  Nobody has answered why it worsens while she follows every instruction.
- **Self-narrative** — she adjusts quietly rather than complaining. "It felt stupid to complain
  about socks." The losses go unmentioned because each one seems too small to raise.

## Real desire

Not pain relief. **Independence, and the refusal to hand it over.**

Surface want: a hip that hurts less. Real want: to keep putting on her own socks, driving
herself, sleeping on her side, reaching her own top shelf — and to not become someone who needs
help showering.

Life Force 8: Freedom from Fear and Pain, plus Comfortable Living. The dominant charge is
regression avoidance — holding what she still has, not aspiring to something new.

The losses are measured in objects and moments, never in pain scores. Dust on the top shelf. The
biscuit tin moved to a lower shelf. Two minutes and a strategy for a pair of socks.

## Blocking belief

**Blocking:** "Nothing works for my hip, and the only real fix is surgery I can't afford, can't
get, or can't take twelve weeks off for."

**New:** "The treatments failed because none of them reached the joint. This one does."

The reframe never attacks the medical system or her doctors. It reframes them as correct but
incomplete — the physio's exercises are good, they just work around the joint. That keeps her
from having to admit she was wrong.

## Sophistication stage

**Stage 4–5.** The category is saturated with heat pads, creams, braces, TENS units and
supplements, and she has tried several. Big claims are dead. What works here is the mechanism
reframe (depth) plus identification — naming her exact evening in her exact words.

## Claims off-limits

*Needs legal sign-off. Nothing here is approved.*

Flags from the briefs worth checking before they run:

- Surgery-cancellation and waitlist-deferral claims — strong, and likely the highest-risk copy
  in the set under AU therapeutic goods advertising rules.
- Specific outcome timelines (week two, week four, week six).
- "$18,000 private / 152 days public" — substantiate the figures.
- "12,000 Australians / 4.8 stars" — must be verifiable.

The briefs already avoid claiming the product treats or cures a condition, and consistently
position it as something used *during the wait* rather than instead of surgery. Keep that.
