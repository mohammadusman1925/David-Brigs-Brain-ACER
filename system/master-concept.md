## 1.1.1 High intent concepts

The thought process comes from intentional, trackable output.

Before anyone writes a script or opens a design file, we need to know exactly who this ad is for, what situation they are in, what emotion we are triggering, and what format we are using to deliver it.

A brief that arrives as a master concept answers five things before production starts:

1. Who is this person and what situation are they in?
2. What is the emotional reason that situation makes them buy?
3. Where are they in their awareness: TOF, MOF, or BOF?
4. How are we delivering it: AI VSL, UGC, Video, or static?
5. What is the specific execution inside the content bucket (the format)?

The loop we run on every briefing:

**Idea, Sprint, Analyze, Iterate or Kill.**

---

## 1.1.2 The master concept, six layers

Every concept runs through six inputs before production starts. Start at the foundation and build up to execution.

```text
6  Format / Framework    specific execution inside the bucket
5  Bucket                AI VSL, UGC, Video or Static
4  Awareness Stage       where is the buyer: TOF, MOF or BOF?
3  Angle                 emotional reason that makes them buy
2  Use Case / Persona    who is this person and what situation are they in?
1  Idea / Hypothesis     what are we testing? the brief.
```

Start with **Use Case** and **Angle**. That is your direction and foundation.

Once those are locked, confirm the **awareness stage**. That drives how the message and the copywriting need to land, what the visual logic should do, and where the landing page sends them.

Then choose **bucket** and **format**. This is where one concept becomes many. The same Use Case and Angle can be delivered as a video, a static, a UGC, or an AI VSL. Each one reaches a different person in a different mindset, on a different day, in a different way.

> One concept executed with intention across multiple buckets will always outperform ten random concepts with no strategic foundation behind them.

## 1.1.3 Use Case and Angle

**Use Case times Angle equals high intent creative output.**

A **Use Case** is the specific situation your customer is in. An **Angle** is the emotional reason that situation makes them buy.

Get both right and you are not making an ad. You are building a relatable situation that triggers a real emotion in a real person. This is what creates MMF, Message Market Fit: the right message to the right person at the right moment.

- **Use Case** = your ICP in a situation
- **Angle** = the emotional reason inside that situation that makes someone buy

### Use Case

The situation. Who this person is and the exact moment they are in. Not a demographic. A real life scene.

Examples from the module:

- Concert goer at a festival
- Musician in rehearsal
- Office worker in a loud space
- Traveller at the airport
- For a skincare brand: woman at the mirror, 7am, skin not improving despite doing everything right

### Angle

The emotional trigger. The reason inside that situation that makes someone stop and buy.

Same use case, four angles:

1. Fear of permanent damage
2. Stay in the moment
3. Knows the damage, still does nothing about it
4. Identity: serious music fans protect their ears

Same person. Same moment. Four completely different emotional triggers.

### The result

A high intent creative brief. Real moments. Real pain points. Written for one specific person in one specific moment. Not a campaign.

Then multiply across buckets: VSL, UGC, Static, Video.

> The angle is the most important thing to get right. It is what makes someone feel the ad was made specifically for them. That is what drives the purchase.

Four to five strong angles hitting the right audience is where 300K days live.

**Nail the angle first. Diversify the execution. Build a dialed in funnel around it.**

Once an angle is working, run it across different formats and buckets to extend its life. Build dedicated landing pages per scaling angle. A lander that speaks directly to that specific problem in the same language as the ad that brought them there.

## 1.1.4 Awareness stages

The awareness stage is the mindset the buyer is in when the ad finds them. It determines how the message needs to be written, what the visual logic should do, and where the landing page sends them.

**Default split: TOF 70 percent, MOF 20 percent, BOF 10 percent.**

### TOF, Top of Funnel, 70 percent of output

They do not know you yet. Engage. Build trust. Create authority. Trigger the angle.

They are not aware of the problem, the solution, or you. The goal is to show them something they did not know they needed, build trust and authority, and get them to spend time with your brand.

TOF is where storytelling is everything. You are not selling. You are pulling someone into a narrative that reflects their life, introduces a problem they recognise, and presents a solution they did not know existed.

What works:

- Long format storytelling
- Educational content that reveals a problem they did not connect to themselves
- Founder ads and authority ads that build credibility
- POV and lifestyle content that creates recognition
- Problem first hooks that pull them in before the product is mentioned
- Use case UGC
- Using authority third party pages
- Whitelisting ads with partners and creators
- Partnership posts

Landing pages: advertorials, educational angle funnels, quiz funnels, dedicated storytelling landers, optimised product page on the angle, listicles.

### MOF, Middle of Funnel, 20 percent of output

They know the problem. They need more trust. Prove the claims.

They are not convinced we are the right answer. The goal is to build the logic bridge. Emotion still carries it but logic can enter now.

**Substage: Solution Aware.** They feel the problem but have not connected it to our solution yet.

- Benefit focused explainers
- Us versus them comparisons
- Demo videos
- Day in the life content
- Value based offers: bundles, value stacks, price anchoring

Landing pages: problem and solution LP, educational advertorial, optimised product page, comparison LP.

**Substage: Product Aware.** They know us but are not sure we fit their specific situation.

- Single UGC testimonial with one clear result
- 3, 5, or 7 reasons format
- Objection busting content
- Use case specific demos with proof
- Decision support offers

Landing pages: product focused listicle, comparison LP, optimised product page.

### BOF, Bottom of Funnel, 10 percent of output

They are ready. Give them a reason to act now.

The goal is to convert. Remove the last reason not to act. Make the decision feel obvious.

- Hard offer ads: discounts, urgency, scarcity
- Bundle creatives
- Review compilations with offer
- Proof heavy demos
- Direct CTA product demos

Landing pages: direct checkout, offer LP, offer PDP, bundle PDP.

### The funnel

TOF, then MOF, then BOF, then convert.

TOF and MOF can overlap instantly through longer formats. That is why you push heavy on TOF. A strong enough TOF ad can take someone from cold to converted in a single session if the storytelling does the full job.

Stop TOF and MOF shrinks. The whole funnel dries up. With enough new people entering consistently, the funnel flows and real scale follows.

### From cold scroll to checkout

The customer spends minutes with the brand across this journey, not seconds.

| Step | What it is | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| TOF | Long format ad, VSL, UGC or video | Engage, build trust, trigger the angle, feel the problem |
| After the click | Pre lander, advertorial or VSL lander | Continue the story, deepen the problem, introduce the solution |
| Product page | PDP or offer LP | Stack the proof, present the offer, remove friction |
| Conversion | Checkout | Express checkout, guarantee visible, zero friction |

What builds inside the customer at each step: trust, then problem aware, then solution aware, then desire, then buying intent, then purchase.

The ad builds the world. The pre lander deepens it. The product page closes it.

### Promo periods

Black Friday. Product launches. Festival season. Mother's Day. These moments change the rules.

During a marketing moment, push BOF harder. Run more of it. Increase the budget. Add scarcity. The audience has a real reason to act now that did not exist last week.

A strong enough offer during a promo period does not just close BOF audiences. It converts cold traffic that would never normally act at this stage. A no brainer offer collapses the funnel into one moment and all three stages convert at the same time.

Default weights outside of promo periods: TOF 70, MOF 20, BOF 10. During a marketing moment, shift the weight toward BOF.

## 1.1.5 Content buckets

Same Use Case. Same Angle. Same Awareness Stage. Four completely different ways to deliver it. Each one reaches a different segment of the same audience.

| Bucket | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| **AI VSL** | Long format storytelling. Time with brand. Hook, reveal, solution, desire. |
| **Video** | Emotional narrative. Movement and depth. Earns time from viewer. |
| **UGC** | Real person, real scenario. Viewer resonates with the creator, not the brand. |
| **Static** | Clear headline. Visual pain point. No clutter. One claim. Clean stop. |

**On AI VSL.** Because production is fast, you can move quickly on long form scripts, test multiple story angles without waiting on shoots, and iterate in days rather than weeks. A well written AI VSL that tells a full story (problem, revelation, solution, proof) can scale an ad account fast. Focus on the script and the storytelling. That is where the performance comes from. People spend more time with your brand. You have the space to build the story, introduce the problem, and make them realise they need the solution.

**On Video and Static.** Strong video makes your whole account perform better, including your statics. When someone has seen your story in a video and then encounters your static, the static lands harder. They already know the brand. The visual just reminds them.

**On Static.** No talking. One strong visual. One clear headline. Show the pain point immediately. Clean, direct, impossible to scroll past.

**On UGC.** Real people, real scenarios, resonating with the viewer's own life. When someone sees a person on screen living their exact situation, dealing with the same problem, and finding a solution that changed something for them, they want that same outcome. It is not an ad anymore. It is someone else's experience that they want for themselves. That is what makes UGC convert in a way brand content cannot. The trust is already there because it is not coming from the brand. It is coming from someone who had the problem first.

### The diversification rule

When a Use Case times Angle wins in one bucket, run it across all four before moving to a new concept.

**Exhaustion is per bucket, not per angle.**

## 1.1.6 Format and Framework

Format is the specific execution method inside the bucket. All the formats used in a static can also be turned into a living video or VSL.

**Static formats:** Before / After, Us vs Them, Comparison, Promo / Offer, Listicle, Reasons, Features, Callout, Press, Social Screenshot, Pain Highlight, Problem / Solution, Myths vs Facts, Testimonial + Review, Star Rating + Count, Cost Comparison, Ingredients / Inside, Bundle Showcase, As Seen In, Question + Quiz, Statistic + Data, Checklist, Split Screen, Urgency / Scarcity, Guarantee Badge, Lifestyle + Text Overlay, Benefit Stack, Warning / Alert Style, Did You Know, Quote Card, Infographic Style, Risk Reversal.

**Video and AI VSL formats:** Demo, Feature Breakdown, Day in the Life, Before / After Story, Transformation Story, Educational VSL, Authority VSL, Founder Story, Founder / Spokesperson, UGC / Actor UGC, Customer Interview, FAQ, Myth Busting, Podcast Format, AI Voice + B Roll, Unboxing, Reaction Video, I Tried X for 30 Days, Tutorial / How to, Multi Creator Mashup, Skit + Comedy, Silent + Text on Screen, Man on the Street, Review Compilation, Whiteboard Explainer, Behind the Scenes, Problem Agitation Solution, News Style / Native Ad, Documentary Style, Talking Head, Animated Explainer, Side by Side Comparison, Why I Switched, Challenge Format.

**Bucket times Format equals controlled diversification.** Same master concept, multiple executions.

## 1.1.7 Pages and sending domains

Most brands only advertise from their own page. That leaves trust, authority, and reach on the table. On Meta you are not limited to your brand page. Each page type serves a different purpose and a different level of trust and reach.

### Your brand page

Trust level: brand credibility.

Clean, intentional, on brand. Represents who you are at every stage of the funnel. Runs all three stages: TOF storytelling and authority, MOF proof and demos and explainers, BOF offers and retargeting and direct close. Not very aggressive claims.

### Third party and authority pages

Trust level: editorial credibility.

Pages you own and manage that are not branded as your product but speak to the audience your product serves. Reads like content, not an ad. Trust is higher because the brand is not the one selling it.

Types that work: lifestyle or wellness editorial pages relevant to your category, best of or comparison style pages, problem specific pages, community style pages that feel organic to the audience.

Best for TOF cold traffic and belief breaking angles.

### Whitelisting

Trust level: personal recommendation.

A creator grants you access to run paid ads from their account through your ad manager. The ad looks like it comes from them. You control targeting, budget, and creative. They keep full account control.

Why it works: the audience sees content from someone they follow and trust. It does not look like a brand ad. It looks like a recommendation from a real person. Whitelisted ads consistently drive 20 percent or more lower CPA compared to standard branded campaigns.

Setup: creator needs a public Facebook page and Instagram set up as Business or Creator, both connected to a Meta Business Manager, creator grants partner access via the Partnership Ads Hub. Once approved you run ads from their handle through your Ads Manager with full targeting.

What you can do with it: run ads from the creator's handle with your own copy and CTA, create dark posts that do not appear on the creator's organic feed, A/B test hooks and angles under the creator's identity.

### Authority pages, what to watch out for

Running ads from a dermatologist, doctor, or specialist page is one of the strongest trust signals. But the authority needs to be real.

If a specialist is recommending your product, that person needs to actually back it. You cannot create a fake endorsement or imply medical credibility that does not exist. Meta prohibits unverified health claims and terms like "clinically proven" or "doctor recommended" without substantiation. Violations lead to legal issues, rejected ads, and account restrictions.

The authority behind the page must be real. The claim must be accurate. No false advertising.

### Why it matters

Your brand page is one voice. Combine it with a creator, an authority page, and a community page and you are speaking to the same audience from three completely different angles of credibility. That expands funnel reach, expands messaging fit, and brings a lot of new people into the funnel.

## 1.1.8 Asset naming

To finish intentional creative strategy, the concepts need to live as trackable assets.

Every concept gets a name before it enters production. The name is what allows you to filter performance by product, bucket, angle, and format inside the ad account. It is how you spot patterns, see what is working, and know where to push next.

**Convention:**

```text
[ProductCode]-[BatchID]-[Bucket]-[Format]-[AngleCode]-[NN/ITE]-[Version]
```

Bucket codes: `VID` video, `STA` static, `UGC` UGC, `VSL` AI VSL.

`NN` is Net New, a brand new concept not tested before. `ITE` is Iteration, a variation of something that already showed signal.

Examples from the module:

- `SP-B047-STA-BFRA-ANG01-NN-V1` equals Silk Pillow, Batch 47, Static, Before/After, Angle 01, Net New, Version 1
- `H1-B031-UGC-TSTI-ANG02-ITE-V3` equals Hears One, Batch 31, UGC, Testimonial, Angle 02, Iteration, Version 3

> If you implement this system the right way, every brief you write has a clear who, a clear why, and a clear how before anything goes into production.
